When two counters describe "the same thing" at different strictness, do NOT assume one bounds the other — verify the relation per repo shape before writing it into a doc, a test, or a reader's head. `honest-graph-counters` framed the earned domain count as a refinement of the raw `cross_anchor_communities`, implying earned <= raw. Measured across six real repos, that holds on multi-package repos (40->10, 20->2, 284->78) and INVERTS on single-package ones: three real single-package repos reported raw 0 with 8, 13 and 20 earned domains.

The mechanism is the `single_dominant` escape: nothing spans two anchors in a one-package repo, so the raw cross-anchor count is 0, while the domain axis deliberately keeps WITHIN-anchor clusters there or a monolithic library would have no domains at all. The two counters range over different candidate sets, so neither bounds the other and an ordering assertion would fail on exactly the repos most people run first (a single-package library).

Generalize: an "X, and X-but-stricter" pair is only nested if both range over the same candidates. Check for an escape hatch or a widening branch in the stricter rule before claiming nesting — and state the non-nesting where a reader meets the fields, not only in the change that discovered it.
