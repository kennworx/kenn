Judge an indexing filter by whether an EDGE disappears, never by whether a literal was skipped. The two differ, and the literal-level number overstates the loss — sometimes to the point of reversing the decision. Concretely, from the SQL code→table pre-filter (crates/kenn-indexer/src/sql/parse.rs, STATEMENT_KEYWORDS): dropping 'lock' and 'analyze' makes the audit report LOCK TABLE x / ANALYZE x literals as misses, which looks like real recall loss and led to both keywords being restored. Re-indexing and diffing the edge set showed 169 distinct edges either way, identical — because a symbol that locks or analyses a table also reads or writes it; that is WHY it locked it. Both keywords were then dropped for good, and both are common English words, so the sweeps they cost were pure waste. The general rule: a literal-level or file-level audit is an instrument, not a verdict. Before adding a keyword, pattern or heuristic back on the strength of one, re-index a real workspace and diff SELECT DISTINCT (src, target, kind) — coverage of the answer is what matters, not coverage of the input.
